# The eight aspects

| # | Aspect | Why it is needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Facts and missing information | Many poor judgments come from treating guesses as facts, or from deciding while key information is still missing. |
| 2 | People involved | A situation rarely concerns only one person. Seeing others' positions and interests is how the situation becomes understandable. |
| 3 | Layers of causes | The same event can have personal causes, causes in relationships, and causes in institutions and structures. Looking at only one layer invites misjudgment. |
| 4 | Field perspectives | Different fields notice different things, and each field usually holds more than one view. |
| 5 | Value questions | Posed as questions rather than answers, so the value judgment stays with the user. |
| 6 | Options and their costs | Several courses of action, each marked by whether it can be undone: a mistake that can be undone is easier to repair than one that cannot. |
| 7 | Uncertainty and limits | AI makes mistakes. Knowing where it may be wrong, and when to turn to a professional, matters as much as the analysis itself. |
| 8 | Questions for you | The final judgment belongs to the user. |
